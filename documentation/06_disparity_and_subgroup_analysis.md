## 2. Inversion of Socioeconomic Gradients: Choice vs. Necessity

A striking disparity identified in this audit is the qualitative inversion of the educational and income gradients between the transportation secure and insecure strata:

### The Educational Inversion
- Among adults **without** transportation insecurity, walking for transportation scales positively with educational attainment: individuals with a bachelor's degree or higher walk significantly more (19.3%) than those with less than a high school education (14.3%).
- In contrast, among adults **with** transportation insecurity, the educational gradient completely reverses: those with less than a high school education walk at a rate of 38.8%, compared to only 26.4% among college graduates.

### The Income Inversion
- Among transportation-secure adults, those in the highest income tier ($\ge 4.00$ poverty threshold) walk at 16.5%, noticeably higher than those in the middle tiers ($2.00$–$3.99$ ratio, which range from 11.8% to 12.3%).
- Among transportation-insecure adults, the poorest individuals ($< 1.00$ poverty ratio) exhibit the highest walking rate (39.8%), while those in higher tiers drop to 21.7%.

### Epidemiological Meaning
This structural pattern reflects the dual nature of transportation walking in the United States:
1. **Elective Walking (Walking by Choice)**: Concentrated among highly educated, higher-income urban residents who walk for short-distance commutes, lifestyle reasons, or leisure transit access.
2. **Compelled Walking (Walking by Necessity)**: Concentrated among low-income, low-education minority populations who walk extended distances along roadway corridors lacking sidewalks because they lack access to a functional private vehicle or reliable public transportation.

## 3. Vulnerability Triad: Race, Urbanicity, and Safety

The subgroup data highlights a critical intersection of vulnerability among Non-Hispanic Black adults with transportation insecurity:
- Non-Hispanic Black adults with transportation insecurity have the highest recorded walking prevalence of any racial group (42.6%), compared to 24.5% among Non-Hispanic White adults with insecurity.
- Yet national pedestrian safety statistics (Smart Growth America, 2024; Sanders & Schneider, 2022) indicate that Black pedestrians are killed by motor vehicles at a rate 82% higher than White pedestrians.
- Consequently, the high prevalence of physical activity observed among transportation-insecure Black adults does not simply represent a healthy lifestyle triumph; it reflects severe exposure to pedestrian injury risk in disinvested suburban and arterial transit corridors.
